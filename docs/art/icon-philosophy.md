# Design philosophy — "Bone & Basil"

A movement about quiet monumentality: the dignity of a small, hand-cut plaque displayed
in a dark chapel. Every surface is treated as carved material — bone, tarnished brass,
sickly moss — lit by a single low candle. The work is not loud; it earns attention
through patient contrast between deep shadow and a few deliberate, luminous strokes.

Space is composed as a reliquary: a single emblem holds the centre, breathing inside a
generous field of near-black green. Margins are sacred. Nothing crowds the frame; the
inset rule and corner ticks mark the boundary the way brass finds mark a coffin lid.
Balance is symmetrical and calm, the stillness of something that has waited a long time.

Colour is a narrow, intentional triad: bone (#eadfc4), tarnished gold (#d9a441), and
moss green (#93b25a), with a whisper of dried blood (#7c2f2a) for memory. No colour is
accidental; each appears once, in its place, calibrated against the others. The palette
must feel gathered from an old world, never chosen from a modern swatch.

Rhythm comes from repetition at the edge of perception: a faint field of tombstones,
grain like dust on glass, a vignette that gathers the eye inward. These marks are made
to reward sustained viewing — you notice them only after you have stopped looking. Scale
is hierarchical: one large gesture, one thin label, one small chip of information, each
sized in exact proportion to its importance.

Craftsmanship is the whole point. Every alignment, kerning, stroke weight and glow is
the product of countless refinements by someone at the top of their field. The final
object should look as though it took many hours of painstaking work and could sit,
without apology, behind museum glass — a small, perfect artifact that treats an
invisible subject with the reverence usually reserved for observable phenomena.
